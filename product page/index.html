Why it matters

The can you had at 4 p.m. is still up at midnight.

Caffeine leaves slowly. For an average adult, about half of it is still around five hours later. Try it with your own afternoon.

What if

See tonight before you drink.

Pick a drink and Last Can draws how it would change your curve. You get one plain sentence: how much you'd have at bedtime, up from how much now.

  • Projection at your own bedtime, in plain language
  • Your new 24-hour total, shown before you commit
  • Nothing is logged until you decide

The awareness bit: the trade-off shows up at the moment you're deciding, not the next morning.

Countdown

A countdown to a calmer night.

Set a level, say 50 mg, and Last Can counts down to when your estimate drops below it. Log another drink and the timer moves right away. When it ends, you get a notification.

The awareness bit: "it wore off" becomes a time you can actually see, and plan around.

Daily limit

400 mg, always in view.

Health Canada's daily maximum for healthy adults is 400 mg. Last Can keeps a rolling 24-hour total against it, warns you as you get close, and asks before you log past it.

The awareness bit: cans, coffee and pre-workout add up quietly. Last Can adds them up out loud.

Personal half-life

Your half-life, not just the average.

Some medications, pregnancy, liver health, age and body size all change how fast caffeine clears. Last Can estimates your half-life from published research and shows exactly what it's based on. Re-evaluate any time.

The awareness bit: the same can doesn't last the same time in everyone.

Privacy

Private by design.

Your drinks, your health conditions and any health data stay on your phone. There's no account server and nothing to sync.

Stays on your phone

Logs, conditions, weight and height are stored on the device and never sent to us.

Health data is optional

Connect Apple Health only if you want to. Last Can then looks for patterns between caffeine, heart rate and sleep, on the device.

Observations, not diagnoses

Anything worth a closer look comes with a suggestion to talk to a doctor. Last Can never says a drink is safe.
